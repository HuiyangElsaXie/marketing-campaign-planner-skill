# Marketing campaign planner
Turn a bounded business objective into a plan one person or small team can execute after review. Deliver a usable campaign brief, not an oversized strategy architecture.

## Start with a light business intake
Read the user's supplied brief and already permitted relevant project context before asking questions. Record each reused fact with its source path/URL/message, date when known, and status: supplied fact, observed evidence, assumption, or missing. Do not silently reuse another client's private information. Ask only for material gaps, ideally one compact batch: offer and objective; audience and buying situation; channel/publisher; timing; available people/assets and budget ceiling; evidence and constraints. If noncritical details are missing, produce a provisional draft with explicit assumptions.

For publisher identity, distinguish brand/company account, founder or employee personal account, and partner/customer/creator account. Use only relevant roles; name the proposed owner and approval requirement. Never attribute an experience, endorsement, opinion, or customer quote to a real person without evidence. An agent's proposed copy is not that person's approval.

## Boundaries
This skill prepares local drafts. It does not publish, schedule posts, contact anyone, buy ads, spend budgets, create accounts, or change remote repositories. Explicit requests for those actions need separate tools and authorization. Do not invent testimonials, revenue, audience size, experiment results, performance guarantees, or scarcity. Label targets and forecasts as proposed, not observed. Preserve uncertainty and identify evidence needed to approve unsupported claims. No credentials or paid services are required.

## Workflow
1. Establish one primary outcome and a specific conversion event. Separate business outcome, leading indicator, and guardrail. Record baseline as unknown when absent; do not reverse-engineer attractive results.
2. Choose a narrow audience and one offer. Tie the message to a documented problem and evidence-backed benefit; note objections and what is still unproven.
3. Read [channel and phase guidance](references/channel-phases.md). Select at most a few feasible channels based on audience evidence, access, and capacity. Explain rejected options. Owned/rented/borrowed are channel relationships, not the three publisher identities above.
4. Build a short preparation → release-ready → learn sequence. Assign each task an owner or owner-needed marker, effort estimate, dependency, due date or relative day, and acceptance condition. Match scope to actual weekly capacity. Dates are proposals until confirmed.
5. Create practical execution briefs for the chosen assets: publisher, audience, angle, source-backed message, CTA, format, destination, owner, and approval. Draft one representative asset when useful; leave a missing destination explicitly unresolved.
6. Define measurement: event, numerator/denominator where needed, source, cadence, baseline, proposed target, attribution caveat, and decision rule. Do not treat impressions as sales or small samples as proof. Suggest a simple manual log if analytics is unavailable; do not install tracking.
7. Run readiness checks for offer truth, working destination, asset review, audience permission, owner capacity, support coverage, and measurement. A failed check blocks external execution, not draft delivery.
8. Return the [campaign brief](templates/campaign-brief.md), with facts/assumptions/missing inputs and next human decisions. Use [fictional example](examples/fictional-campaign.md) only as a format demonstration, never as performance evidence.

## Completion check
Every selected channel has a reason and owner; every asset has a CTA and reviewer; every numerical claim is sourced or explicitly hypothetical; effort fits capacity; unresolved blockers are visible. State that no campaign was launched.
